## Introduction

Every journey into computer science begins with a single question, a broken script, or curiosity about how software actually works under the hood. 

This write-up documents the early curiosity, the first languages explored, and the foundational projects that eventually led to a focus on security engineering, cryptography, and systems programming.

> *“The only way to learn a new programming language or discipline is by building programs in it.”*

## The Early Spark

Starting out in programming often means experimenting with basic syntax and small scripts before understanding architecture or systems design. The transition from writing simple programs to understanding data structures, network communication, and operating system internals marked a turning point in how I viewed technology.

## Evolution Towards Cybersecurity

What began as general interest in software engineering quickly deepened into a fascination with security boundaries:
- How does data flow across untrusted networks?
- How are cryptographic guarantees established and maintained?
- Why do systems fail, and how can they be architected to resist adversaries?

These questions led directly to projects like **SYNQ** (exploring the Signal Protocol, X3DH, and Double Ratchet) and research in **post-quantum cryptography**.
